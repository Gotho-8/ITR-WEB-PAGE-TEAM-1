Introduction to the Respiratory System

The respiratory system is the group of organs responsible for carrying out the exchange of gases (oxygen and carbon dioxide) between the environment and the blood. This process is essential for cellular life and the proper functioning of all other systems in the human body.

Complete Anatomical Structure

The respiratory apparatus is composed of upper and lower respiratory tracts working together:

1. Nasal Cavities

They are the main entrance. They filter dust, moisten the air, and warm it up using the nasal mucosa.

2. Pharynx

A muscular tube that connects the nose to the larynx. It serves as a pathway for both air and food.

3. Larynx

It contains the vocal cords that allow us to speak and features a flap called the epiglottis to keep food from entering the lungs.

4. Trachea

A strong and flexible cartilaginous tube that guides clean air directly into the chest area.

5. Bronchi

The trachea divides into two main branches (bronchi) that enter each lung, splitting into smaller and smaller airways.

6. Lungs and Alveoli

The main spongy organs. Inside them, the alveoli (tiny air sacs) perform the rapid exchange of oxygen into the bloodstream.

Diseases of the Respiratory System

There are several pathologies that can obstruct or damage the airways, making cellular oxygenation difficult:

Chronic Asthma

Causes the airways to swell and narrow, leading to wheezing, shortness of breath, and coughing.

Lung Cancer

The development of malignant tumors that destroy healthy lung tissue, frequently caused by smoking or environmental pollutants.

COPD

Chronic Obstructive Pulmonary Disease. It causes a persistent blockage of airflow, severely damaging the alveoli.

How robotics helps

Robotic technology directly helps fight and correct respiratory diseases by offering a level of accuracy that the human eye and hand cannot achieve alone. Specifically, robotics plays a key role by helping to perform ultra-precise internal surgeries in the lungs to remove tumors without damaging the surrounding healthy tissue, which shortens patient recovery times. It also helps by acting intelligently through automated systems capable of regulating the exact amount of oxygen a patient needs in intensive care, and it even assists through the design of programmed microdevices that travel through the airways to clear obstructions caused by asthma or mucus directly.
